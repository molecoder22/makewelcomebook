## 2. Is the listing broken? No — it is outranked

Two tests from a logged-out browser (important: when logged in as the shop
owner Etsy boosts your own listing in your own results, which fooled us on
2 Sep — that reading was wrong):

- Search **"welcome book no canva"** → our listing does **not** appear.
- Search **"12 question welcome book wifi qr"** → our listing **does** appear.

So the listing is indexed and eligible. It only surfaces for phrasing that
almost exactly repeats our own title — i.e. queries no real buyer types. For
every query a buyer would actually use, we are nowhere.

That is the expected outcome for a shop with 0 sales and 0 reviews. Etsy ranks
overwhelmingly on sales velocity and review count.

## 4. The flywheel we are outside of

Etsy ranks on sales and reviews → we have none → we get no views → we get no
sales → we get no reviews. This is a closed loop. It does not open on its own,
and no amount of title/tag/photo work opens it. Only two things do:

1. **Paid placement** (Etsy Ads — unlocks ~17 Sep), or
2. **Traffic we bring ourselves** (our own ads/site pointed at Etsy).

Everything else — better title, better photos, better tags — only decides
whether a view converts. It cannot create the view.

## 5. On adding downloadable templates

Mário asked whether we should ship actual downloadable files. Findings:

- **Our delivery method is NOT the problem.** SparkBranding's bestseller
  delivers "4 PDF" files that simply contain Canva links. Delivering a PDF that
  links onward is the category norm, not a red flag.
- **But every competitor delivers something the buyer edits and keeps.** We
  deliver access to a web app. For a buyer comparing listings, "62 designs,
  finished for you" is harder to picture than "4 editable Canva files".
- **Our real differentiator is unclaimed by anyone: no Canva required.** Every
  single competitor on page 1 requires a Canva account. We are the only
  finished-result product. That is genuinely distinct.
- **However** — nobody searches "no canva". It is a great reason to buy once
  you are on the page, and useless for getting onto the page.

**Recommendation:** adding a small pack of ready-made printable PDFs (e.g. a
WiFi sign set + house-rules + checkout card) as a genuine downloadable extra is
worth doing — it makes "what you get" concrete and supports a second listing —
but it will not fix visibility, and it should not be mistaken for the fix.

## 6. Does it make sense? — honest verdict

**Yes to one more test, no to open-ended spending.**

The case *for*:
- The product works. Both buyers used it deeply (one session of 1 h 22 m).
- The unit economics are fine: ~€14–15 net per Etsy sale.
- The shop, listing, photos and files are already built and paid for. The
  marginal cost of testing is only the ad spend.
- Etsy Ads clicks in digital templates are normally far cheaper than the
  €2.28–2.90 we were paying on Google. Cheaper traffic changes the maths.
  (Unverified for us — that is exactly what the test measures.)

The case *against*:
- We would be entering the most crowded, most price-eroded corner of Etsy at
  2–3× the median price with zero reviews.
- Realistic expectation for a ~€60 Etsy Ads test: **1–5 sales**, i.e. roughly
  €14–75 back. Break-even at best.

**So the honest reason to run it is not the return — it is that it is the only
way to buy the first reviews, and reviews are the thing that unlocks
everything.** If it produces 3+ sales and 1–2 reviews, the flywheel starts and
a second test is justified. If it produces 0–1, the answer is settled and we
stop.

## 7. The one decision that materially changes the odds: price

At $20.30 with no reviews we are asking a stranger to take the biggest risk on
the page. Options:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| **A. Hold $29 / $20.30 on sale** | Protects margin and positioning. Lowest conversion. Slowest to first review. |
| **B. Launch price ~$12–14 until 10 reviews** | Puts us inside the page's price band. Best chance of first reviews. Net drops to ~€8–9/sale. |
| **C. Cut to ~$8** | Matches the cheap end. Wins on price, destroys the premium story and the economics. |

**My recommendation: B, temporarily.** Not because the product is worth less —
because the first ten reviews are worth more than the margin on the first ten
sales. Revert to $29 once reviews exist. This is Mário's call: it is money.

## 8. What to do regardless of the price decision (free)

1. **More listings.** SparkBranding has 105; we have 1. Each listing is another
   entry point. Cheap ($0.20 each) and the single best free lever.
2. **A downloadable extras pack** (WiFi sign / house rules / checkout card PDFs)
   to make "what you get" concrete and to support listing #2.
3. **Landing page: lead with the finished example.** Two independent sources
   said this — the site heatmap (example button beat the primary CTA 3:2) and
   the Google sitelinks (the example link out-clicked *and* out-converted
   "Start Your Book Free").
