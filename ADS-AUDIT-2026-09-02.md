## 2. Where the money went wrong: the "guest book" problem

Roughly **€24 of the €54.66 (44 %) was spent on searches for physical guest books** —
the paper books wedding and holiday-home guests sign. Zero of those clicks converted,
and they never could: we sell a printable welcome/house-manual book, not a signing book.

Wasted search terms included: *guest book · short term rental guest book · accommodation
guest book · accommodation guestbook · bnb guest book · custom guest book for vacation
home · event guest book · guest book for home / holiday home / guest house · a guest book*.

Two independent signals confirm this is an intent mismatch, not bad luck:

- **Auction insights** — the advertisers we compete against most are Etsy (43.6 % of our
  impressions), Amazon (17.9 %), Papier (15.6 %) and Artifact Uprising (11.2 %). These are
  physical guest-book and photo-book sellers. When your rivals are stationery shops, you
  are in the wrong auction.
- **Quality Score** — `[vacation rental guest book]` carried a low-quality-score flag: Google
  itself judged our landing page a poor answer to that query. It had 57 impressions,
  5 clicks, €9.67 and 0 sales.

**Fixed today** (2026-09-02): campaign negatives `"guest book"` and `"guestbook"` (phrase
match) added, and `[vacation rental guest book]` + `[vacation rental guest book template]`
paused. `[guest information book]` and `[guest welcome book]` survive — they do not contain
the contiguous phrase, and one of them is a genuine buyer term.

**Expected effect:** removing €24 of dead spend from the same 2 sales moves CPA from
€27.33 to roughly **€15**, i.e. from −€4 to about **+€8 profit per sale**. (Caveat: about
€31.91 of spend sits in Google's privacy-hidden "other search terms" bucket, so the exact
saving cannot be verified until we see a clean week of data.)

## 3. Devices: mobile was burning 40 % of the budget

| Device | Impr. | Clicks | Cost | Sales | CVR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop | 107 | 11 | €28.27 | 1 | 9.1 % |
| **Mobile** | **315** | **11** | **€21.66** | **0** | **0 %** |
| Tablet | 7 | 2 | €4.73 | 1 | 50 % |

Mobile produced 73 % of impressions, 40 % of the cost, and **zero sales in eleven days**.
That is logical: our product is a 12-question builder that ends in a printable PDF. Nobody
sets up their rental's welcome book on a phone at a bus stop; they do it at a desk.

**Fixed today:** mobile bid adjustment set to **−50 %**. We still appear on mobile (people do
research on phones and buy later on a laptop), but we stop paying premium prices for it.
Tablet and desktop untouched.

## 5. Can we scale? — the honest answer is *no, not much*

This is the most important finding in the report, and it is not about budget.

**Budget is not the constraint.** Impression share lost to budget: **2.6 %**. Impression
share lost to rank: **62.4 %**. We are only capturing 35.1 % of the searches we are eligible
for, and the reason is ad rank (bid × quality), not money running out. The daily budget of
€16 has never once been binding. **Raising the budget would buy literally nothing.**

**The real ceiling is search volume.** Keyword Planner, US, 12-month averages:

| Keyword | Monthly US searches | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| airbnb welcome book | 390 | −46 % |
| airbnb signs | 390 | — |
| airbnb welcome book template | 260 | −64 % |
| airbnb guest book | 260 | — |
| vacation rental guest book | 210 | — |
| airbnb house manual template | 140 | — |
| airbnb house manual | 90 | — |
| airbnb guidebook template | 70 | — |
| airbnb welcome guide | 70 | +29 % |
| airbnb welcome letter | 70 | — |
| airbnb wifi sign | 50 | — |
| welcome book for airbnb | 50 | — |
| vacation rental welcome book | 40 | — |
| guest welcome book | 40 | — |
| …plus a long tail of 10–20/month terms | | |

Stripping out the guest-book terms that are not our market, **relevant US demand is roughly
1,200–1,400 searches per month.** That is the entire pool. Even in a fantasy scenario —
100 % impression share, our current 5.6 % CTR — that is about **75 clicks/month**, and at an
8.3 % conversion rate, **about 6 sales/month**.

Note the trend column, too: the two biggest terms are down 46 % and 64 % year-on-year.
This niche's Google demand is shrinking, not growing.

**Realistic optimised ceiling for Google Search:**

| Scenario | Clicks/mo | Sales/mo | Spend | Net revenue | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (before fixes) | ~65 | 5–6 | ~€150 | ~€130 | **−€20** |
| After today's fixes | ~40–55 | 5–8 | ~€75–120 | €117–187 | **+€40–70** |
| Theoretical maximum | ~75 | 6–7 | ~€170 | ~€160 | ≈ break-even |

Note the last row: pushing for 100 % impression share is *counter-productive*, because the
extra inventory is the low-intent stuff we just excluded. **This campaign's job is to be a
small, reliably profitable trickle — about €40–70 profit per month — not a growth engine.**

## 6. Verdict and recommendations

**Does it make sense to keep running?** Yes — but only as a small, disciplined channel.
After today's changes it should make money rather than lose it, it validates that people
buy the product, and it feeds the conversion data we need. Keep it at €16/day (it will
naturally spend €4–6).

**Can we scale it?** No. Not with more budget, and not with more bidding. The market on
Google Search is ~1,300 searches/month and shrinking. Any real growth has to come from
somewhere other than Google Ads.

to lift revenue per sale. Google Ads is a floor, not a ladder.
